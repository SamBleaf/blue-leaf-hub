# Legal and Usability Critique

## Legal focus

This pack is a strong starting point, but it must not be treated as final legal advice.

### Strengths

- It uses fixed templates rather than free AI text.
- It separates management, site and worker audiences.
- It requires SWMS before high risk construction work.
- It includes monitoring, review and worker sign-on.
- It uses versioning and generated document snapshots.
- It links risk triggers to SWMS, permits, inspections, registers and toolbox talks.
- It includes consultation records, which are often missed.

### Legal gaps to review before live use

1. Clause references need to be confirmed against current SA WHS Regulations and relevant Codes of Practice.
2. The list of high risk construction work should be mapped directly to the WHS Regulations.
3. SWMS content should be checked by a competent WHS consultant.
4. Asbestos workflow needs strict review, especially for renovation work.
5. Silica controls need review against current crystalline silica requirements.
6. Confined space, crane and electrical work may require specialist contractor documentation rather than builder-authored SWMS.
7. Incident notification workflow must identify notifiable incidents and preserve incident sites where required.
8. Worker consultation process should be operational, not just documented.
9. Subcontractor SWMS review process needs clear responsibility.
10. Site-specific changes must force review and re-approval.

## Usability focus

### What tradies will actually use

Tradies are unlikely to read a full WHS Management Plan onsite.

They will use:

- site board
- emergency contacts
- site rules
- relevant SWMS
- toolbox talk
- permits for the task
- QR induction

### Design rules

1. Worker-facing pages should be short.
2. Use icons and headings in rendered PDFs.
3. Put emergency details at the top of site-facing documents.
4. Avoid long generic legal paragraphs in worker documents.
5. Keep SWMS task-specific.
6. Let supervisors generate documents from one screen.
7. Use QR codes for induction, SWMS and site board.
8. Do not ask supervisors questions the Hub already knows.
9. Do not force free-text where a dropdown works.
10. Flag missing items clearly.

## Recommended approval workflow

1. Draft generated.
2. Supervisor reviews project-specific data.
3. Director or WHS responsible person approves.
4. Output becomes locked PDF.
5. Workers sign on.
6. Any material profile change marks output stale.
7. Revised version is generated and reissued.

## High-risk warning

Replacing HazardCo removes the safety net of a third-party managed content library. Blue Leaf must own:

- template review
- legislation monitoring
- code updates
- incident process
- training
- consultation
- subcontractor document review

That is acceptable if managed properly, but dangerous if the templates become stale.
