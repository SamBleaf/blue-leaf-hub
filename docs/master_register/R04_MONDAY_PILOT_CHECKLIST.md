# Master Register — Monday pilot, one-page checklist

For the staff member running the pilot. Plain steps, in order. You are working on **one named pilot lead only** —
if a lead has no register card, it is not a pilot lead; tell Sam, don't improvise. Nothing you do here emails or
invites a real client unless the step says so and you choose it.

---

## Before you start (30 seconds)
- [ ] Open the pilot lead in **Sales → Pipeline → (the lead)**. You should see a **Register** button in the lead
      workspace. If you don't, stop — it isn't switched on for this lead.
- [ ] You are on a normal work laptop in Chrome/Edge. No special setup.

## 1. Capture the brief, room by room  (the main job)
- [ ] Open **Register → Rooms**. Work **one room at a time** using the room dropdown (or **Save & next room →**).
- [ ] For each room, answer the **lead questions shown first** (e.g. *"New kitchen or retrofit?"*, *"Layout &
      family use"*). These are the few that matter most for that room.
- [ ] Need more detail? Open **"Explore more"** under a heading — nothing is hidden or lost, it's just tucked away
      so the page reads like a conversation, not a form.
- [ ] Set each item's **disposition** honestly: *New / Alter / Existing to remain / Make good / By others /
      Excluded / Undecided*. **"Undecided" is a valid answer** — leave it rather than guessing.
- [ ] Don't type a price anywhere. The register records **decisions and scope**, not costs.
- [ ] Glance at **"This room at a glance"** at the foot of each room — it lists what's agreed and what's still to
      confirm. Use it to see what's missing before you move on.
- [ ] Hit **Save** (or **Save & next room**). Wait for the save to confirm — if it says it couldn't save, it
      **didn't** save; re-open and try again, don't assume.

## 2. Review before anything goes out
- [ ] Open **Register → Documents**. You'll see draft briefs (client brief, architect brief, selections schedule).
- [ ] **Preview** each one. Read it as the client would. It should read as plain decisions, not jargon.
- [ ] If a draft is wrong, go back to the room and fix the source item — the documents regenerate from the brief.

## 3. Issue only what you mean to  (the deliberate step)
- [ ] **Issuing is a decision, not a side effect.** A selection stays invisible to the client until you issue it.
- [ ] Issue a document from the **Documents** step. The app records it as *"Recorded P01 for <client> — not
      emailed."* **Recording ≠ sending.** It will only email a client if you explicitly choose to, and **in the
      pilot you do not email or invite a real client.**
- [ ] A selection you send to the client can come back as **Accept** or **Request changes** — those are different
      outcomes. *Request changes* is **not** an approval; it means you have something to resolve first.

## 4. Baseline and handover
- [ ] When the brief is agreed, record a **baseline** (Register → the baseline action). This locks a reference
      version you can compare against later.
- [ ] Check the **Handover** panel. It shows five separate facts — **contract signed**, **baseline recorded**,
      **project status**, **Ops-ready handover**, and **portal stage** — plus **one clear next action and who owns
      it**. Don't read one as another (a recorded baseline is not a signed contract).
- [ ] The client is **never auto-invited**. Inviting is always a deliberate click, done per lead.

## If something looks wrong
- [ ] **Don't push through it.** Screenshot it and send to Sam with the lead name and what you clicked.
- [ ] Red flags to report immediately: a client sees something you didn't issue; a save says success but the data
      isn't there on reload; the handover panel contradicts what you know is true; any price appears.
- [ ] Kill-switch is Sam's: unsetting the pilot flag makes the register disappear instantly, with no data touched.

## What NOT to do in the pilot
- ❌ Don't email or invite a real client.
- ❌ Don't enter real costs or payment details.
- ❌ Don't use it on a lead that has no register card.
- ❌ Don't treat "Undecided" or "Request changes" as finished — they're open items.
